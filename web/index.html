Inside

Past the door,
the chain sees nothing.

Cella is a shielded pool. The chain stores only sealed commitments. Your balance exists as notes that only your wallet can open, and every move is checked by a zero-knowledge proof made on your own device.

Trust

Trust the proof,
not the operator.

Most private-payment designs put an operator in the middle who keeps the ledger and could read it, freeze it or lose it. Cella has no such party. The contract accepts a movement of value only when a proof shows it is yours to move.

Who keeps the ledger?
Nobody. There is no server. Your balance is rebuilt from the chain with nothing but your wallet.
Who can move your funds?
Only a proof made with your key. Not the owner, not the auditor, not a relayer.
Can withdrawals be paused?
No. The owner can pause new deposits, never transfers or withdrawals.
Can the contract be changed?
No. There is no upgrade path.

Auditor

Private,
not unaccountable.

One named key can read every transfer: who paid whom, and how much. The proof itself forces each transaction to seal a true copy for that key, so a sender cannot hide from the auditor.

The auditor can read. It can never spend, and replacing it takes seven days in plain view.
